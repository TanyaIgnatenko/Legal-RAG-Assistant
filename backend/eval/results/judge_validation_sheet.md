**Question:** Who gets to judge whether a country beyond the bloc is safe enough to send records to?

**Answer:**

## 2

**Question:** Am I able to pick up everything one provider holds on me and hand it over to a rival service?

**Reference:** Yes, where processing rests on consent or a contract and is automated. The data must be supplied in a structured, commonly used, machine-readable format, and may be sent directly between providers where technically feasible.

**Answer:**

> Yes, the data subject has the right to receive the personal data concerning them—which they have provided to a controller—in a structured, commonly used, and machine-readable format, and have the right to transmit those data to another controller without hindrance, provided that:
> (a) the processing is based on consent pursuant to point (a) of Article 6(1) or point (a) of Article 9(2), or on a contract pursuant to point (b) of Article 6(1); and
> (b) the processing is carried out by automated means.
> 
> Additionally, the data subject has the right to have the personal data transmitted directly from one controller to another, where technically feasible.
> 
> Citation: CHAPTER III - Article 20, paragraphs 1 and 2

## 5

**Question:** How fast does a firm have to raise the alarm with the regulator once it realises its systems were compromised?

**Reference:** Without undue delay and where feasible within 72 hours of becoming aware. A later notification must explain the delay. Notification is unnecessary if the breach is unlikely to risk individuals' rights and freedoms.

## 6

**Question:** If someone believes a company mishandled their details, can they go to court directly or must they raise it with the watchdog first?

**Reference:** Either route is open and neither blocks the other. A person may lodge a complaint with a supervisory authority, typically where they live, work, or where the infringement occurred, and separately has a right to an effective judicial remedy against the controller or processor before the courts of the Member State where it is established or where the person resides.

## 10

**Question:** After systems are compromised, how does telling the authorities differ from warning the people affected?

**Reference:** The regulator must be told whenever a breach is likely to pose a risk, without undue delay and where feasible within 72 hours. Affected individuals must be told only where the risk to their rights and freedoms is high, and then without undue delay; that communication can be avoided by encryption, by later measures removing the high risk, or by a public announcement where individual contact is disproportionate.
